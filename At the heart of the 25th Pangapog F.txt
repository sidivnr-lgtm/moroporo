At the heart of the 25th Pangapog Festival at Freedom Park in Peñaplata, Island Garden City of Samal, Sama women proudly showcase the vital role they play in agriculture and community livelihood.
Amid the colorful celebration of Samal’s culture and abundance, these women prepare, process, and sell agricultural products, including banana-based goods harvested from the island’s fertile lands. Their work reflects the daily efforts of women who contribute not only to their families’ income but also to the local agricultural economy.
As members of the Sama tribe, their participation carries a deeper meaning. Agriculture and traditional livelihoods are closely connected to their cultural identity and the resources provided by their environment. Through their hands, knowledge, and dedication, they help transform the island’s harvest into food, income, and opportunities for their communities.
This photograph captures more than women selling products—it portrays women as active contributors to agriculture, culture, and community development. Their presence at Pangapog demonstrates how traditional knowledge continues to thrive while creating opportunities for future generations.
The scene is a celebration of the Sama women whose labor sustains livelihoods and whose resilience keeps Samal’s agricultural heritage alive.
